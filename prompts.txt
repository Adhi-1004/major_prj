Essential Conditions for On-Screen Dialogue [Researched and got it from searching on browser]

To classify dialogue as on-screen, the scene must meet these specific conditions:

Visual presence: The character's body or face is inside the camera frame.
Sync accuracy: The mouth movements perfectly match the audio track.
Camera alignment: The speaker is in the active, current shot.
Real-time action: The speaking occurs during the live timeline of the scene.

When to Label Dialogue as "On-Screen"

You explicitly label or treat dialogue as on-screen in scripts and captions under these circumstances:
Standard scenes: The camera focuses directly on the person talking.
Mirror reflections: The character speaks while visible through a reflective surface.
VFX screens: The character appears speaking via a live video call or television broadcast inside the scene.
Cutaways: The camera stays on the speaker instead of cutting to the listener.
When It is NOT On-Screen (The Contrast)

Understanding the opposites helps clarify the definition:

Off-Screen (O.S.): The character is in the room, but the camera is pointed at something else.
Voice-Over (V.O.): The speaker is narrating from a different time or place, or it is a internal monologue.
To help you apply this correctly, tell me how you plan to use this information:

Other People in the Frame
Background actors: Extras can walk, eat, or move around the scene.
Reacting characters: Other main or supporting actors can be visible in the same shot, listening or reacting.
Overlapping speakers: Multiple people can be on screen speaking simultaneously, such as in a chaotic argument.

Other Audio Elements
Diegetic sound: Real-world noises within the scene, like traffic, wind, or music playing from a radio.
Non-diegetic sound: Elements added in post-production, like a dramatic musical score or sound effects.
Cross-talk: Background chatter from crowds or other characters murmuring nearby.

As long as the camera shows the specific person whose mouth is moving to deliver the specific lines of dialogue, it is legally on-screen dialogue.
